# Making a vague request precise

## The one-sentence version

Requests arrive vague on purpose or by accident, and the two or three questions you ask to sharpen them are the highest-value thing you do all day.

## Why requests are vague

- The person does not know exactly what they want until they see options.
- They are describing a feeling ("churn seems high") not a measurement.
- They have a solution in mind and are asking for that instead of the problem ("pull me the list of accounts that churned" when they actually want to know *why* churn rose).
- They assume context you do not share ("the usual cut" means nothing to a new analyst).

None of this is the requester being difficult. It is just what an unrefined request looks like.

## The clarifying questions

Most requests get sharp with answers to some subset of these:

- **Compared to what?** "High" needs a baseline: last month, last year, the target, the other regions, the plan.
- **Over what period?** Last 7 days, last quarter, year to date, a specific month.
- **For whom?** All customers, or a segment. New or returning. One product line or the blend.
- **At what grain?** One number, or per-region, or per-week. This is the "shape" from chapter 2.
- **What will you do with the answer?** The single most useful question. If they will make a go/no-go call, you need a clean yes/no with a confidence range. If they are exploring, a broader cut is fine. The decision determines the analysis.
- **How precise does it need to be, and by when?** A rough directional answer this afternoon is a different job from a defensible number next week.

You rarely need all six. Two or three, chosen for the specific request, usually do it.

## The XY problem

Someone asks for X (a specific output) because they think it will get them to Y (what they actually need). Often X is the wrong path and you can see a better one, but only if you ask "what are you trying to figure out?" instead of just producing X.

"Can you pull everyone who logged in fewer than 3 times last month?" -> "Sure. What are you trying to work out?" -> "Whether low early usage predicts churn." -> that is a completely different, better analysis, and now you can do the right one.

## The move

Before starting, send back a one-line restatement: "To confirm: you want churn rate by plan tier, monthly, for the last 12 months, and you are deciding whether to change the entry-tier pricing. Right?" Either they say yes and you have a precise brief, or they correct you and you just saved a day. Do this every time. It never looks like over-caution; it looks like competence.
