# What is the one insight

## The one-sentence version

Every analysis should collapse to a single sentence that a listener could repeat correctly the next day, and if you cannot write that sentence, you are not ready to present.

## What it is

The **one insight** is the single most decision-relevant thing you found, stated as a claim, not a topic. It has three properties:

- **It is a sentence with a verb**, not a subject. "Month-2 users who never open feature X churn at 3x the rate" is an insight. "Churn analysis" is a topic. "Retention" is a category.
- **It implies an action.** If someone hears it, they know roughly what to do or ask next.
- **It is specific and quantified.** "3x", "month 2", "feature X", not "some users churn more".

Everything else in your presentation exists to support, contextualise, or qualify that one sentence.

## Why it exists

An audience remembers one thing from a meeting, sometimes zero. If you have not decided what that one thing is, the audience picks for you, and they usually pick wrong (a striking-looking chart, an offhand comment, the last slide). Choosing the one insight deliberately, and building everything around it, is how you control what they walk out believing.

## How it works

**Finding it:** after the analysis, before the deck, ask "if I could only tell them one thing, what would it be?" Write it. Then stress-test:

- **Is it a claim?** Rewrite anything that is a noun phrase into a sentence.
- **So what?** If the answer is not obvious, the insight is too shallow. "Sales are down 4%" -> so what? -> "Sales are down 4% entirely because of one region; the other five grew." Now it points somewhere.
- **Is it true and defensible?** Could a skeptic knock it down in one question? If so, either it is not your insight or you need the supporting slide ready.
- **Could the listener repeat it tomorrow?** Say it out loud. If it takes 30 seconds and three clauses, tighten it.

**The pyramid.** Once you have the one sentence, the presentation is:

1. The insight (say it first, on slide 1, in the title).
2. The 2 to 4 pieces of evidence that support it, one per slide.
3. The recommendation that follows from it.
4. Backup: method, alternatives considered, caveats.

You are not building up to the insight like a mystery. You state it, then prove it. Executives especially want the conclusion first and the reasoning available on demand.

**When there are two insights:** pick one to lead. A presentation with two co-equal headlines has no headline. If both genuinely matter, make one the main story and the other a clearly-labelled secondary finding, or split into two presentations.

**When there is no insight:** sometimes the honest finding is "we looked and nothing is going on" or "the data cannot answer this yet". That is a legitimate one sentence. Say it plainly and say what would be needed to get an answer. Do not manufacture a story from noise to have something to present.

## When you use it

The moment the analysis is done and before you make anything. It is the hinge between "I found things" and "I will communicate this". If you skip it, you build a deck that wanders.

## A worked example

An analyst spends a week on a support-tickets dataset and has: volume trends, channel mix, resolution times by agent, a seasonality pattern, a correlation between ticket length and satisfaction, and a cluster of angry tickets about one feature.

Ten findings. The one insight, after asking "so what" of each: **"40% of last quarter's negative reviews trace to one billing bug that support cannot fix, only apologise for; engineering has it as a P3."**

That sentence has a verb, a number, a cause, and an implied action (repriotise the bug). The deck becomes: the insight on slide 1, then a slide showing the 40% breakdown, a slide showing the bug's ticket volume over time, a slide with three verbatim customer quotes, and a recommendation to move the bug to P1. The seasonality and channel-mix findings go in the appendix; they are true but they are not the story.

> **Try This**
> For a completed case, write your one insight as a single sentence with a verb and a number. Say it out loud. Then ask "so what" once and see if the sentence gets sharper. That sentence is your slide 1 title.
